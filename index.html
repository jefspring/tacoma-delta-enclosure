Long walls run the full length; the end walls fit between them. Walls sit on the base rather than beside it, so the base carries the load in compression.

Why 3/8" is enough. Thin marine ply with epoxy fillets is dinghy construction — far stronger than the number suggests, and it saves roughly 8 lb over 3/4" throughout. Finished weight lands near 22 lb.

03Ventilation

Two Noctua NF-A14 industrialPPC-3000 PWM — 140 mm, 158.5 CFM, 10.52 mmH₂O static pressure, 6.6 W each. The high static pressure is the reason for this fan rather than a quieter one: air has to move through louvres, a shutter track and a filter.

LocationHeight above interior floor
IntakeFront long wall, centred on length4.5" — low

Diagonal cross-flow that works with convection rather than against it. Cutouts are 5.0" (127 mm) diameter with mounting holes on a 124.5 mm square pattern.

Sizing check

Worst case is the DELTA charging at 800 W while inverting for the Wave 2 air conditioner — roughly 150 W of waste heat.

Q = 3.16 × W ÷ ΔT°F → 3.16 × 150 ÷ 12 = 40 CFM for a 12 °F rise.

Even derated 50% by the louvres you have better than double the margin, so run the fans on PWM at partial speed and keep them quiet. Add a Demciflex 140 mm magnetic dust filter on the intake only — positive internal pressure then keeps dust out of the exhaust side.

Control with a W1209 or Inkbird ITC-306 thermostat: on at 85 °F, off at 75 °F, fed from the aux 12 V circuit rather than from the DELTA itself.

04Sealed ↔ ventilated conversion

Three layers at each opening, giving a box that converts from fully sealed to freely ventilated without tools.

  1. Exterior — louvred marine vent. Slats angled down and out. This alone sheds driving rain.
  2. Middle — the fan.
  3. Interior — sliding shutter panel in a UHMW or HDPE track, 7.6 × 7.8 × 1/4", closing onto a 1/8" EPDM gasket seat.
The detail that makes it seal. Cut the shutter track with a 1/32" taper over the last 2" of travel so the panel wedges hard against the gasket as it closes. That is the difference between a cover and a seal, and it is a router-table job. Simpler alternative: two rare-earth magnets pulling the panel onto the gasket.
Fit a Gore PolyVent pressure-equalisation plug high on one end wall. With the shutters closed the box is otherwise airtight, and a sealed wooden box through a Nebraska temperature swing becomes a condensation trap. The Gore vent passes air and water vapour while blocking liquid water. It is the difference between "sealed" and "sealed and dry".

05Foam and shock protection

Minicel T200 cross-linked closed-cell polyethylene, 2 lb/ft³ — the whitewater kayak outfitting standard. Zero water absorption, tough, cuts clean, holds its shape indefinitely.

Adhesive: DAP Weldwood Contact Cement, the solvent original rather than the water-based version.

The ribs are the important detail. They sit at the four corners of the long walls and touch the DELTA only at its corners, leaving the centre of each long face wide open for airflow. Full-face foam on those walls would suffocate the unit. The 2" lid pads capture it vertically when the lid latches, so between base, ends, ribs and lid pads the DELTA is held in all six directions with no strap required.

Design the cables to live through the glands permanently. The XT150 to the alternator charger and the 10 AWG AC patch both exit one end wall through glands, so normal operation never requires opening the box. The lid is for removing the unit, not for daily use.

08Build sequence

  1. Cut all six panels. Dry-fit before any epoxy touches anything.
  2. Cut the two 5.0" fan openings, the gland holes and the Gore vent hole while the panels are flat.
  3. Rout the shutter tracks, including the 1/32" taper over the final 2" of travel.
  4. Seal every cut edge and both faces of every panel with penetrating epoxy, 2–3 coats.
  5. Assemble walls to base; fillet all interior joints with thickened epoxy.
  6. Fair, prime and paint the exterior. Two coats of topside paint.
  7. Bed and fit the louvred vents, glands, Gore vent, hinge and latches in butyl.
  8. Fit fans, guards, filter and thermostat. Bench-test airflow direction before closing up.
  9. Cut and glue foam: base slab, end pads, corner ribs, lid crush pads.
  10. Apply the EPDM gasket to the wall rim, butting the ends. Set the unit, run the cables, latch down.

09Two things to check before cutting plywood

Siting. The fan walls need 3–4" of clear air on both sides. If either long face ends up tight against a bed side or the propane compartment, the louvres are decorative and the entire ventilation design fails. Lay this out in the drawer plan first.
Weight. The finished enclosure adds roughly 22 lb, taking the permanent rear-axle load from about 167 lb to 190 lb. Mount it forward of the rear axle centreline, where weight splits between both axles instead of levering off the rear.
Design spec for a personal build. Verify all part numbers and dimensions against your own measurements before cutting or ordering — every truck bed and drawer system differs.
