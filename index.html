03Ventilation

The unit's own fans are on the long faces. Both 19.6 in sides are vent grille almost end to end. Those two 2 in plenums are its lungs — no penetrations anywhere on the long walls above the intake slot band, and nothing may sit tight against them internally.

Intake — passive, no fan

Three slots per long wall, 6 × 0.875 in each, in a band centred 2.44 in above the interior floor. Roughly 16 in² per side against the ~14 in² that 40 CFM needs at 400 ft/min, so about 2× margin.

Slots on both long walls deliberately: if one face ends up tight against a bed side, the other still feeds. A passive slot needs an inch or two of standoff, not the 3–4 in a fan face would demand — which is the siting problem that finally disappears.

Each slot gets an external drip hood so the actual air entry faces downward. Driving rain has to turn a corner and climb to get in.

Exhaust — one lid fan under a rain cap

A single Noctua NF-F12 industrialPPC-3000 PWM, 120 mm, 110 CFM, 4.2 W, lying flat in the 2.5 in top plenum. A 120 mm axial is only 25 mm thick, so it fits the plenum easily when it blows vertically.

Above it: a coaming ring standing proud of the lid so surface water cannot run into the opening, then a raised cap on three standoffs. Air leaves radially under the skirt; nothing falls in vertically. The exit annulus is roughly 33 in² against a 15.9 in² fan opening, so the cap is not a restriction.

Why this beats every earlier revision

Cool air enters low, hot air leaves high. Buoyancy now pushes the same direction as the fan instead of fighting it, so the fan can run slower for the same result and there is still passive airflow if it ever fails.

Q = 3.16 × W ÷ ΔT°F → 3.16 × 150 ÷ 12 = 40 CFM for a 12 °F rise at the worst case of 800 W charging while inverting for the Wave 2. One 110 CFM fan derated 50 % by the cap and louvres still clears it.

04Screen access and the power button

The screen end carries the LCD, four USB-A, two USB-C, a USB ON/OFF button and the main power button. Nothing plugs in here in normal use, so this end gets the tighter 2.0 in gap — shorter plunger throw and closer viewing.

The window does not open

1/4 in cast acrylic (Plexiglas G, not extruded — better craze resistance), rabbeted in from the inside so water pressure seats it onto the gasket rather than lifting it off. Permanently bonded. A sliding seal on a vertical weather-facing surface is the hardest thing in the whole box to keep dry; bonding the pane removes the problem entirely.

The buttons do not need an opening either

Sealed boot-plungers. A silicone pushbutton boot bonded over a hole, with a delrin plunger inside bridging the gap to the unit's button. Press the boot, it presses the button. Nothing opens, nothing seals and unseals.

Leave a 3–4 mm rest gap. If the foam settles or the unit shifts, a zero-clearance plunger could hold a button down permanently. The gap is what makes this safe.

The sliding element survives, with a different job

A sliding storm shutter rides over the fixed window. Because the seal is behind it, the shutter does not need to be waterproof at all — it guards the acrylic from UV and gravel. You keep a sliding panel, doing something it is actually good at.

Know this before you rely on the window. The manual notes the LCD switches off after a single press and the unit hibernates with the screen dark after 5 minutes of inactivity. A window alone shows you a dark screen most of the time — you press the power button to wake it, then read it. That makes the boot-plunger essential rather than a nicety, and it means the EcoFlow app is your real at-a-glance state of charge. You already need the app for AC timeout and AC-Always-On.

05The power end

Six AC sockets, the X-Stream AC inlet, the solar/car XT60, DC5521 outputs, car socket, AC ON/OFF and 12V DC ON/OFF all live on this end. It gets the wider 3.5 in gap for plug bodies and cable bend radius, and it carries the cable glands — the shortest possible run to every connector.

These are permanent connections. Wire them once and the box never needs opening for normal use: the AC patch to the drawer distribution and the XT150 to the alternator charger both live through the glands.

06Foam and shock protection

Minicel T200 cross-linked closed-cell polyethylene, 2 lb/ft³ — the whitewater kayak outfitting standard. Zero water absorption, tough, cuts clean, holds shape indefinitely.

No hinge is deliberate. A hinge fixes which way the lid opens and adds a second sealing interface to get right. With four latches and a removable lid you can lift it off in whichever direction the drawer bay allows, and the gasket sees even clamping pressure all the way round.

09Build sequence

  1. Cut all six panels. Dry-fit before any epoxy touches anything.
  2. Measure your own unit first. From a fixed datum — the bottom of the case — record the centre height of the LCD, its width and height, and the centre of the power button. Five measurements. Everything else in this box tolerates a quarter inch; a window and a plunger bore do not.
  3. Cut the window rabbet, the plunger bores, the 4.5 in lid fan opening, the intake slots and the gland holes while the panels are flat.
  4. Rout the shutter track and the intake damper tracks, including the 1/32 in taper over the final 2 in of travel.
  5. Seal every cut edge and both faces with penetrating epoxy, 2–3 coats.
  6. Assemble walls to base; fillet all interior joints with thickened epoxy.
  7. Fair, prime and paint the exterior. Two coats of topside paint.
  8. Bed and fit the drip hoods, glands, Gore vent, coaming, cap and latch pads in butyl.
  9. Bond the acrylic pane and the boot-plungers. Check plunger alignment and rest gap with the unit in place before the adhesive cures.
  10. Fit fan, guard and thermostat. Bench-test airflow direction before closing up — a fan installed backwards is invisible until the box cooks.
  11. Cut and glue foam: base slab, four corner ribs, two lid crush pads.
  12. Apply the EPDM gasket to the rim, butting the ends. Set the unit, run the cables, latch down.

10Weight and siting

Weight. The finished enclosure adds roughly 24 lb, taking the permanent rear-axle load from about 167 lb to 191 lb. Mount it forward of the rear axle centreline, where weight splits between both axles instead of levering off the rear.
Siting is no longer critical. With passive intakes and a lid exhaust, the long walls need an inch or two of standoff rather than 3–4 in of fan clearance, and both short ends need only enough room to reach the connections. This was the constraint that drove three earlier revisions.
Design spec for a personal build. Verify all part numbers and dimensions against your own measurements before cutting or ordering — every truck bed and drawer system differs.
