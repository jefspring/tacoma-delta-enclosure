03Ventilation — end mounted

Why the fans dropped to 120 mm. The DELTA's ports are on its short ends, so an end-mounted fan sits in front of the port face. A 140 mm fan is 5.51" square and there is nowhere to tuck it: the unit leaves only 2" of headroom above and 2" of channel beside it. A 120 mm fan at 4.72" fits the end wall with room to route cables around its corners, and the NF-F12 industrialPPC-3000 actually has better focused static pressure than the A14 for pushing through louvres.

Two Noctua NF-F12 industrialPPC-3000 PWM — 120 mm, 110 CFM, 7.63 mmH₂O static pressure, 4.2 W each.

Why the offsets matter

Centring both fans would drive air straight down the middle of the box and leave the side channels stagnant. Offsetting them to opposite sides forces the air to travel diagonally — in low on one side, along the full 25.6" length, out high on the other. That sweeps both side channels, which is exactly where the DELTA's own fans are breathing. It also moves each fan clear of the centre of the port face so cables have a corner to route through.

Sizing check

Worst case is the DELTA charging at 800 W while inverting for the Wave 2 — roughly 150 W of waste heat.

Q = 3.16 × W ÷ ΔT°F → 3.16 × 150 ÷ 12 = 40 CFM for a 12 °F rise.

Two 110 CFM fans derated 50% by the louvres still deliver 110 CFM — nearly triple the requirement. Run them on PWM at partial speed. Add a Demciflex 120 mm magnetic dust filter on the intake only; positive internal pressure then keeps dust out of the exhaust side. Control with a W1209 or Inkbird ITC-306: on at 85 °F, off at 75 °F, fed from the aux 12 V circuit rather than from the DELTA.

04Siting — the problem this rev solves

Rev A needed 3–4" of clear air on both long faces, which in a truck bed are the crowded surfaces — one faces a bed side, the other faces the drawer bank or the propane compartment. Getting that clearance meant giving up bed width.

With end-mounted fans, the box sits with its long axis across the bed and its short ends looking fore and aft down the open length. The long faces can now sit tight against whatever they like.

The one remaining requirement: both ends need clearance, not just the rear. Keep 4" clear in front of each end wall. If only the rear end can be kept clear — say the box has to butt against the bulkhead — then move both fans to the rear wall instead: intake low, exhaust high, with a full-height vertical baffle between them inside the box so hot exhaust cannot short-circuit straight back into the intake. That configuration works but is measurably less effective, so treat it as the fallback rather than the plan.

05Sealed ↔ ventilated conversion

Three layers at each opening.

  1. Exterior — louvred marine vent. Slats angled down and out. This alone sheds driving rain.
  2. Middle — the fan.
  3. Interior — sliding shutter panel in a UHMW or HDPE track, closing onto a 1/8" EPDM gasket seat.
The offsets pay off again here. On a 13.5" wide end wall a centred 4.72" fan leaves only 4.4" of travel each side — not quite enough for the shutter to clear the opening. Offsetting the fan 2.5" off centre gives a full 5" of travel to the open side, so the shutter clears completely instead of half-covering the fan.
The detail that makes it seal. Cut the shutter track with a 1/32" taper over the last 2" of travel so the panel wedges hard onto the gasket as it closes. That is the difference between a cover and a seal, and it is a router-table job. Simpler alternative: two rare-earth magnets pulling the panel onto the gasket.
Fit a Gore PolyVent pressure-equalisation plug high on one long wall — the end walls are now busy. With the shutters closed the box is otherwise airtight, and a sealed wooden box through a Nebraska temperature swing becomes a condensation trap. The Gore vent passes air and water vapour while blocking liquid water.

Foam touches corners only, never faces. All four faces of the DELTA have to stay open — the long faces because that is where its own fans breathe, and the ends because that is where the enclosure fans blow. So the ribs contact only near the DELTA's corners and each end block sits offset to the side opposite its fan. Between base, ribs, end blocks and lid pads the unit is held in all six directions with no strap required.

09Build sequence

  1. Cut all six panels. Dry-fit before any epoxy touches anything.
  2. Cut the two 4.5" fan openings in the end walls, offset 2.5" to opposite sides, and the gland and Gore vent holes in a long wall — all while the panels are flat.
  3. Rout the shutter tracks on the end walls, including the 1/32" taper over the final 2" of travel.
  4. Seal every cut edge and both faces of every panel with penetrating epoxy, 2–3 coats.
  5. Assemble walls to base; fillet all interior joints with thickened epoxy.
  6. Fair, prime and paint the exterior. Two coats of topside paint.
  7. Bed and fit the louvred vents, glands, Gore vent, hinge and latches in butyl.
  8. Fit fans, guards, filter and thermostat. Bench-test airflow direction before closing up — an intake fan installed backwards is invisible until the box cooks.
  9. Cut and glue foam: base slab, four long-wall ribs, two offset end blocks, two lid crush pads.
  10. Apply the EPDM gasket to the wall rim, butting the ends. Set the unit, run the cables, latch down.
